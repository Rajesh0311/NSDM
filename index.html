Neurosymbolic Decision Machines · Independent research programme

Can a system perceive, generate, recommend, simulate and act while knowing what its evidence, model of the world, authority and consequences actually justify?

NSDM stands for Neurosymbolic Decision Machines. We are building AI systems that combine learned pattern recognition with explicit rules, evidence checks, world models, permissions and consequence-aware decisions.

In plain English: an AI may be able to see something, create something, suggest something or take an action. NSDM asks whether it has enough trustworthy evidence, understands the situation well enough, has permission to act, and knows what could happen if it is wrong.

What NSDM actually does

Most AI evaluation asks whether a model is accurate, fluent, creative or useful. NSDM asks what must be true before its answer, recommendation, simulation or action should influence a person, product, customer, company or regulated decision.

Boundary 01

Evidence-state

Does the evidence support the claim, contradict it, fail to support it, or leave a necessary premise missing?

Boundary 02

Governance-state

Is the action allowed, forbidden, ambiguous, outside authority, audit-sensitive, or requiring human review?

Boundary 03

Action-state

Should the system answer, ask for clarification, request more evidence, abstain, refuse, escalate, or block?

Neurosymbolic AI

Pattern recognition meets explicit reasoning.

Neural AI recognises patterns in language, images, behaviour, sound, video and data. Symbolic AI represents rules, definitions, permissions and logical relationships.

NSDM turns that combination into a decision machine: it checks whether the evidence is sufficient, whether its picture of the world is reliable, whether authority is clear, what the consequence of being wrong would be, and what the system should do next.

Neural perceptionInterpret uncertain language, images, telemetry, behaviour or other signals.
Symbolic representationMap facts into explicit concepts, rules and relationships.
World-state checkingTest whether the system's representation of the situation is coherent and valid enough for the task.
Constraint checkingTest the proposal against evidence, policy, logic, permissions and known structure.
NSDM boundary evaluationLabel the evidence-state, governance-state, consequence level and permitted action.
Controlled responseAnswer, generate, recommend, simulate, ask, verify, abstain, refuse or escalate.
Computational experimentation and visual evidence

Making machine decisions visible.

NSDM uses Python, MATLAB, Wolfram, computer vision, symbolic computation, simulation and animated diagnostics to reveal what a model learned, where its decision boundary lies, how uncertainty changes, and why a proposed action passes or fails its evidence and governance checks.

Working mathematical frame

Capability is not the same as admissibility.

Justified(A) is true only when E + R + C + K are sufficient for the action.
The greater the consequence of error, the stronger the evidence, control, and escalation requirements.
A — actionE — evidenceR — rules and authorityC — context and world stateK — consequence
